# Birds over the valley

Research for the bird obstacles (MECHANICS.md §6b) and their Field Notes facts. Checked 2026-11-01.

## Roster: who is really around Hot Springs
Source for presence: the NPS species checklist for Hot Springs National Park (HOSP), birds:
https://irmaservices.nps.gov/v3/rest/npspecies/checklist/HOSP/Bird

| In game | Park checklist | Season used in game | Season source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chimney swift | Present | spring, summer | Arrive in April, leave for South America in October (MDC) |
| Turkey vulture | Present | all year | Soars on thermals (MDC) |
| Blue jay | Present | all year | Year-round resident; harvests acorns in fall (NPS oaks article) |
| American crow | Present | all year | Year-round resident |
| Canada goose | Present | fall, winter | Migrating flocks in spring and autumn (MDC) |
| Cedar waxwing | Present | winter, spring | Common spring and fall migrant, winter flocks on cedar berries (MDC) |
| Bald eagle | Present | winter | Winter is the best time to see them in Arkansas (AGFC) |

Dropped after checking:
- **Mississippi kite.** Not on the park checklist, and I found no Garland County record.
- **American white pelican.** Not on the park checklist. The only Lake Hamilton record I found is a 1930s note in *The Auk*, which I could not open to verify.

Night levels have no birds, because these species roost at night.

## Facts worth adding (sourced)
- Blue jays carry up to 5 acorns at once, bury up to 5,000 a season at a depth just right for germination, and recover only about 1 in 4. This helped oaks spread north after the ice retreated.
  NPS, Species Spotlight: Oaks. https://www.nps.gov/articles/species-spotlight-oaks.htm
- Turkey vultures soar on rising warm air with their wings in a V and tilt from side to side. Wingspan is about 70 in.
  Missouri Department of Conservation. https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/turkey-vulture
- Chimney swifts look like "flying cigars" and catch insects in flight. They arrive in April and leave for South America in October.
  Missouri Department of Conservation. https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/chimney-swift
- Cedar waxwings travel in large, quiet flocks and eat cedar berries in winter. Their tails have bright yellow tips, and their wings have red tips like sealing wax.
  Missouri Department of Conservation. https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/cedar-waxwing
- Migrating Canada geese fly in V-shaped chevrons. Males give a long, loud "a-honk!"
  Missouri Department of Conservation. https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/canada-goose
- Arkansas's first modern bald eagle nest was documented in 1983. The AGFC now estimates 150–160 nesting pairs, against a recovery goal of 10. Winter is the best time to see eagles, with DeGray Lake one of the best-known spots.
  Arkansas Game & Fish Commission. https://www.agfc.com/news/eagles-inspire-awe-appreciation-for-conservation-efforts/
